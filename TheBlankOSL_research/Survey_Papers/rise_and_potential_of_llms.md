# The Rise and Potential of Large Language Model Based Agents: A Survey

## 1. Introduction

**Goal:** To understand AI agents’ development, particularly how Large Language Models (LLMs) are driving innovation towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

**LLMs as the Foundation:** LLMs like GPT-3, GPT-4, etc., are key to creating AI agents due to their advanced reasoning, language understanding, and generalization capabilities. LLMs could serve as a core framework to build agents that interact, learn, and adapt to complex environments.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Origin of AI Agents

- **Philosophical Roots:** The idea of an agent involves autonomy and intentional action, drawn from classical philosophical ideas.
- **Introduction to AI:** In AI, an agent perceives, reasons, and acts based on input from its environment. AI agents differ from philosophical agents as they don’t necessarily possess true consciousness or desires.

### 2.2 Technological Trends in Agent Research

- **Symbolic Agents:** These early agents used logical rules but struggled with large-scale, real-world applications.
- **Reactive Agents:** Focused on direct input-output actions, they respond quickly to stimuli but lack complex reasoning.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Agents:** RL enables agents to learn from their environment, leading to successes like AlphaGo. However, RL systems require large data and are inefficient for real-world applications.
- **Transfer Learning and Meta-Learning:** These techniques help agents generalize knowledge across tasks but have limitations in complex or diverse tasks.
- **LLM-based Agents:** LLMs exhibit advanced reasoning, planning, and language generation. These agents can interact with the environment and perform tasks without needing task-specific training.

### 2.3 Why LLMs are Ideal for AI Agents

- **Autonomy:** LLMs can act independently, responding to inputs and making decisions.
- **Reactivity:** Multimodal inputs (text, visual, audio) allow LLM-based agents to react swiftly to their environment.
- **Proactivity:** They can generate goal-oriented actions and respond to long-term objectives.
- **Social Ability:** LLMs can communicate with humans and other agents effectively, leading to collaboration or competition.

## 3. Construction of LLM-Based Agents

A general framework is introduced with three key components:

### 3.1 Brain

The brain processes information, stores memories, reasons, plans, and makes decisions. LLMs serve as the brain, capable of high-level reasoning, long-term planning, and handling diverse tasks.

**Key Features:**

- **Natural Language Interaction:** LLM-based agents can engage in multi-turn dialogues, generate high-quality text, and understand implied meanings behind human instructions.
- **Knowledge:** LLMs store vast linguistic, commonsense, and domain-specific knowledge, but issues like outdated or hallucinated information can arise.
- **Memory:** Efficient memory systems store past interactions, and models like GPT-4 are designed to handle complex, multi-step reasoning tasks.
- **Reasoning and Planning:** Chain-of-Thought (CoT) techniques improve step-by-step reasoning, enabling agents to handle complex tasks.
- **Transferability and Generalization:** LLM-based agents can generalize across unseen tasks through methods like in-context learning and continual learning.

### 3.2 Perception

Perception expands beyond text to include multimodal inputs (visual, auditory, etc.), improving the agent’s ability to understand and interact with the world.

### 3.3 Action

LLM-based agents can output text, use tools, and take embodied actions. This allows them to interact with environments, solve problems, and even shape their surroundings.

## 4. Agents in Practice: Real-World Applications

### 4.1 Single-Agent Scenarios

- **Task-Oriented Deployment:** LLM-based agents perform well in executing specific, focused tasks, such as answering questions or generating code.
- **Innovation-Oriented Deployment:** These agents can suggest creative solutions and help in research, design, or planning.
- **Lifecycle-Oriented Deployment:** Agents can be applied to tasks that require long-term planning, adaptability, and continued learning.

### 4.2 Multi-Agent Systems

- **Cooperative Interaction:** Multiple agents can collaborate on tasks like problem-solving, negotiation, or resource management.
- **Adversarial Interaction:** Agents may also compete, simulating environments where different objectives are pursued.

### 4.3 Human-Agent Interaction

- **Instructor-Executor Paradigm:** Human users give instructions, and agents execute them.
- **Equal Partnership:** More advanced scenarios where humans and agents collaborate on equal footing.

## 5. Agent Society: Emergent Social Behavior

### 5.1 Behavior and Personality

LLM-based agents can exhibit social behaviors and unique personalities. This can influence their interaction patterns in multi-agent systems or with humans.

### 5.2 Environment for Agent Society

Agents can operate in various environments (text-based, virtual, or physical), enhancing their ability to simulate real-world social dynamics.

### 5.3 Simulated Society

Agent societies simulate human social structures. These can offer insights into social behaviors but also pose risks, such as bias or ethical concerns.

## 6. Discussion

- **LLM Research and Agent Development:** LLMs and agent research benefit each other, driving improvements in both fields.
- **Evaluation and Trust:** The performance of LLM-based agents is measured by their utility, sociability, and adaptability. Trustworthiness, especially concerning hallucinations and adversarial attacks, remains a major concern.
- **Scaling Up:** Challenges arise when scaling the number of agents, especially concerning coordination, interaction, and control in large agent societies.

## 7. Conclusion

LLM-based agents represent a promising step toward Artificial General Intelligence. They have vast potential applications, but challenges remain in terms of scalability, ethical risks, and the development of a truly autonomous, adaptable agent society.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rapid Evolution:** LLM-based agents are rapidly evolving and have significant potential for autonomous decision-making, multi-agent cooperation, and human collaboration.
- **Versatile Applications:** These agents can be used for various real-world applications, from simple task execution to complex social simulations.
- **Challenges:** Ethical risks, hallucination issues, and ensuring that agents are trustworthy and secure are significant challenges.
- **Advancing Capabilities:** Understanding and expanding the scope of perception (beyond text) and action (embodied, multimodal) are critical to advancing agent capabilities.

This survey gives a broad perspective on how LLM-based agents could transform AI, providing a foundation for future developments towards AGI.
